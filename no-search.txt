Nice options exist under 300 EUR, but the best pick depends on what kind of coffee you want (espresso, milk drinks, or pod convenience) and how involved you want to be in the process. Here’s a practical short-list in Europe-friendly categories, with quick pros/cons and typical price ranges.

Best overall value (semi-automatic, great espresso control)
- De’Longhi Dedica EC685
  - Why it’s good: slim, 15-bar pump, decent steam wand for lattes/macchiatos, good build for the price.
  - Typical price: around 180–250 EUR.
  - Best for: users who want real espresso and milk drinks but still under budget.

Solid budget semi-automatic options
- De’Longhi ECP3330 / ECP3320 family (15-bar pump, traditional frother)
  - Pros: straightforward, reliable, easy to maintain.
  - Cons: no advanced features, but excellent value for basic espresso.
  - Typical price: about 120–180 EUR.
- Krups XP3440 / similar Krups pump machines
  - Pros: affordable, easy to use, decent steam wand on most models.
  - Cons: build quality varies by model; features are basic.
  - Typical price: around 100–170 EUR.

Best bean-to-cup / all-in-one (still under 300)
- Philips 2200 Series (examples: EP2231/EP2220/LatteGo variants)
  - Pros: built-in grinder on some variants, one-touch drinks on others, good milk systems with LatteGo.
  - Cons: some models don’t have the same grind quality as pricier bean-to-cup machines; sometimes you pay extra for the LatteGo version.
  - Typical price: around 199–299 EUR depending on model and promotion.
  - Best for: convenient filter-coffee-to-espresso with milk drinks, without extra fuss.

Pod/capsule machines (easy and fast)
- Nespresso Essenza Mini
  - Pros: tiny footprint, superb espresso quality for the price, very low maintenance.
  - Typical price: 100–160 EUR.
  - Best for: quick espresso with minimal cleanup.
- Nespresso VertuoPlus (or similar Vertuo line)
  - Pros: can do both coffee and espresso with pod system; larger variety of pod sizes.
  - Typical price: 149–199 EUR.
  - Best for: a bit more coffee variety and convenience while staying under 300.
- Lavazza A Modo Mio Jolie (or similar Lavazza pod machines)
  - Pros: good-value pods, compact.
  - Typical price: around 100–150 EUR.
  - Best for: a simple pod-based setup with decent espresso quality.

Dolce Gusto / versatile pod options
- De’Longhi / Dolce Gusto Genio S Plus (Dolce Gusto system)
  - Pros: very easy, wide range of pods (hot chocolate, tea, coffee, etc.), compact.
  - Typical price: around 90–140 EUR.
  - Best for: households wanting beverage versatility and ease of use.

Notes and quick buying tips
- If you want real espresso and are okay with some manual effort, a semi-automatic like the Dedica EC685 is hard to beat under 300.
- If you prefer zero-fuss drinks and milk frothing, a bean-to-cup with LatteGo or a good espresso pod system (Nespresso Vertuo/Essenza Mini) is very convenient.
- Pod machines save time and cleanup but can cost more per cup and you’re limited to pod formats.
- Check for:
  - 15-bar pump standard in most budget espresso machines (used to pressurize water for crema).
  - Milk frothing option you actually like (steam wand vs. built-in milk system like LatteGo).
  - Build quality and warranty in your country.
  - Availability of spare parts and accessories locally.
